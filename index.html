2 · What is a model?

A model squeezes data into a few parameters

A regression replaces hundreds of data points with a handful of numbers. You can throw the y-values away and regenerate them from those numbers and the x-values, approximately. Whatever does not come back was lost.

This is the entire model.

4 · The decompression test

Give it only a title. It writes the whole article.

If the model compressed Wikipedia, it should be able to decompress it. We asked it for the complete article on each sampled topic, from the title alone, and fact-checked every sentence against the real article. Then we scaled up to every eligible English topic (lists, disambiguation pages and very short articles are left out), the way a poll scales up from a sample.

    5 · The definition test
    
    
    A closer look: just the opening paragraph
    
    
    Same topics, a smaller task: write only the article's first paragraph, about 120 words. Short texts let us measure meaning, wording, dates and facts in detail, and compare with a paraphrase of the real text. Similarity tells us whether the rewrite is on topic, not whether its facts are right. For the facts, see the sentence-by-sentence check in section 4.

      7 · What the model finds surprising
      
    
      Cheap words are already in the model
      
    
      The model reads the real article one token at a time and guesses the next one. Bits measure its surprise: 1 bit is a coin flip, 10 bits is a 1‑in‑1,000 guess.
      
    
      
      
      
        
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
    
      
    
      Pale tokens cost almost nothing: the model already knew them. Dark tokens are what it could not predict: a fact it lacks, or just an unusual choice of words. Add up the bits and you get the compressed size of the text.
      
  
      

      



      
  
      
    
      8 · Made-up topics
      
    
      Ask about something that does not exist
      
    
      We invented titles that sound real and checked that none exists on Wikipedia. Then we asked for the article.
      
    
      
    
      
    
      Hallucination is extrapolation. Like a curve predicting far outside its data, the model fills the gap with something plausible, in the same confident tone.
